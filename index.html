How to play HEXAKU

HEXAKU is a block puzzle played on a honeycomb of 61 hexagonal cells. It borrows the familiar rhythm of wood block puzzles, but changes the one rule that matters most: on a square grid a line can only run two ways, across or down. A hexagon has three pairs of parallel sides, so here a line can run three ways. That third direction is the one new players keep missing, and it is where the game hides most of its depth.

The basic loop

  1. Three blocks sit in the tray. Drag one onto the comb. Blocks never rotate, so working out where each shape fits is the puzzle.
  2. Fill every cell of a line — in any of the three directions — and it bursts.
  3. When all three blocks are placed, three new ones arrive.
  4. The game ends when none of the remaining blocks fit anywhere.

Scoring and combos

Your score is the number of cells you clear, multiplied by your combo. Combo counts lines, not turns: clear three lines at once and you are immediately at 3. Clear one more on the next turn and you are at 4. The multiplier climbs exponentially, so a single long chain is worth vastly more than the same lines cleared separately. Place a block that clears nothing and the chain resets to zero.

Rare tiles

Eraser

You start with three. Wipes seven cells and does not take a tray slot. No score, but no penalty either — your combo survives. Save them for when the board gets tight.

Colour

Destroys every tile sharing the colour of the cell you drop it on, anywhere on the board.

Chain

Rare. Spreads outward through every tile touching the one you hit, like a chain reaction.

Blast

Rare. Destroys all three lines running through a single cell, and counts as three lines of combo.

Five things good players do

  • Watch the rim. Lines near the edge are only five cells long; lines through the middle are nine. The rim is where you go for a quick clear, the middle is where you set up something worth setting up.
  • Never break a chain for a small gain. At combo 8 the multiplier is already over thirteen. Losing that to clear one short line is almost always a mistake.
  • Plan all three blocks before placing the first. The order you place them in changes what fits afterwards more than most people expect.
  • Hold your rare tiles. A Blast used at combo 2 is worth a fraction of the same Blast used at combo 8.
  • Leave the centre breathing. Once the middle is congested, the long lines become impossible and the board tends to collapse quickly.

FAQ

Is HEXAKU free?

Yes. It runs entirely in your browser with no download, no account and no payment.

Does it work on mobile?

Yes. The layout adapts to portrait and landscape, and the whole game is drag-based, so touch works exactly like a mouse.

Is my progress saved?

Yes. Your current board, score and best score are stored locally in your own browser, so you can close the tab and continue later from the same device.

Does it work offline?

Once the page has loaded, yes — the whole game is a single self-contained file with no external requests.
